The problem with drawing on your screen

Every screen drawing app works the same basic way: press a shortcut, and a transparent sheet appears over your screen to draw on. The trouble is that the sheet is glued to the screen, not to what you drew on.

So you circle a button in Safari, drag Safari aside to show something else, and the circle is now floating over your desktop. Press ⌘Tab to answer a question in Slack and your Safari notes are now scribbled across Slack. Most apps deal with this by making you erase and start again, or by fading your ink away after a few seconds.

Other apps

Drawings stay at a fixed spot on the screen. Move the window or switch apps and they point at nothing.

JellyDraw

What Smart Window Anchoring does

JellyDraw's Smart Window Anchoring attaches each drawing to the app window you drew it on. In practice that means:

  • Drag a window and its drawings come along, at 60 frames per second, staying exactly on the thing you circled.

  • Switch apps freely. Your Safari notes stay on Safari, your Xcode notes stay on Xcode, and each set is still there when you switch back.

  • Windows stack the way you'd expect. If another window slides over an annotated one, it covers that window's drawings too, instead of the ink floating on top of everything.

  • Minimize a window or move it to another Space and its drawings go with it. Close the window and they're cleared.

  • Need the whole screen? Press Tab to draw anywhere across every display, or switch to a whiteboard or blackboard.

JellyDraw only needs macOS Accessibility access to do this, which it uses to read where your windows are. It never reads what's inside them and never records your screen.

Questions

Is there a Mac app that keeps screen drawings attached to a window?

Yes: JellyDraw. Its Smart Window Anchoring attaches each drawing to the window under it, so the drawing moves with the window and hides and reappears with it when you switch apps. As of October 2026 we haven't found another Mac annotation app that does this; the others draw on a transparent layer fixed to the screen.

What's the best free app for drawing on the screen on a Mac?

JellyDraw's free version includes the pen, highlighter, eraser, select and move, undo and redo, whiteboard and blackboard, drawing on every display, and window anchoring, with no time limit. ZoomIt for Mac and DrawPen are also free and open source, but their drawings stay fixed to the screen. All the free options.

How do I draw on my screen during a Zoom, Meet or Teams call?

Install a screen annotation app like JellyDraw, share your entire screen rather than a single window, then press ⌃⌥⌘Space and draw. When you share just one window, most meeting apps capture only that window and leave overlays out. Step-by-step guide for Zoom, Meet and Teams.

Does macOS have a built-in way to draw on the screen?

Not live. macOS Markup works on screenshots, PDFs and images after you've captured them. To draw over apps while you're presenting, you need a separate app.

Does JellyDraw record my screen or collect data?

No. It asks only for Accessibility access, to read the position and size of the window you're drawing on. It never reads what's inside your windows, doesn't need Screen Recording permission, and has no analytics or tracking. Drawings stay in memory on your Mac until you clear them or export a copy.

Does JellyDraw work on Intel Macs or older macOS versions?

No. JellyDraw needs macOS 26.2 (Tahoe) or later on a Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 or newer). On an older Mac, Annotate, Scribbble and Presentify support earlier versions of macOS.

Do drawings follow the content when I scroll?

Not yet. Window anchoring follows the window itself: moving it, switching apps, minimizing and changing Spaces. Scrolling inside the window doesn't move the drawings.
