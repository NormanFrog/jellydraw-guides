Free ways to draw on your screen on a Mac

You don't need to pay to circle, underline and sketch over your Mac's screen. Here's every genuinely free option, what each one is good at, and which "free" apps are really trials.

By the JellyDraw team · Updated October 23, 2026 · We make JellyDraw, one of the apps below. Other apps' details come from their own websites, linked in each section.

Quick answer

JellyDraw is the best free option for drawing while you present, teach or record. The free version never expires, and its drawings stay attached to the window you drew them on.

  • Need to zoom in? ZoomIt for Mac is free and open source.
  • Also on Windows or Linux? DrawPen is free and open source on all three.
  • Just marking up a screenshot? macOS Markup is built in.

Free apps with no time limit

1. JellyDraw: best for presenting, teaching and recording

Free forever · optional Pro $9.99 once · macOS 26.2+ on Apple Silicon

Press ⌃⌥⌘Space from any app, draw, and press Esc to get back to work. Your drawings attach to the window under them: drag the window and they move with it, switch apps and each app keeps its own notes.

Free includes
Pen, highlighter, eraser, select and move, undo and redo, whiteboard and blackboard, and window anchoring. No watermark, no trial
Pro adds
Rectangles, ovals, lines, text, PNG/SVG export and accent colors, for $9.99 once
Install
Download, drag to Applications. Asks only for Accessibility access
Missing
Zoom, cursor spotlight and auto-fading ink. Doesn't run on Intel Macs or before macOS Tahoe 26.2

2. ZoomIt for Mac: best free zoom tool

Free and open source, from Microsoft · macOS 14+

The Windows presenter classic, now on the Mac. It's built around zooming into part of the screen, with drawing, typing, screenshots, OCR, screen recording and a break timer on top.

Install
Homebrew, from Terminal (instructions). Needs Screen Recording permission
Good for
Technical demos where zoom matters more than drawing
vs JellyDraw
Does far more besides drawing, but its drawings don't follow windows. Full comparison

3. DrawPen: free on Mac, Windows and Linux

Free and open source (MIT) · Apple Silicon download or Homebrew

DrawPen is a simple transparent overlay with a brush, fading pen, shapes, text, highlighter, laser pointer, eraser and whiteboard. ⌘⇧A switches between drawing and using your apps.

Good for
People who want one free tool on every operating system
vs JellyDraw
Has shapes and text for free, but drawings stay fixed to the screen and it isn't built just for the Mac

Free options already on your Mac or in your meeting app

macOS Markup (for screenshots, not live drawing)

Press ⇧⌘4, select an area, then click the thumbnail that appears in the corner. Markup opens with a pen, shapes, text and a magnifier, and you can share the result straight away. It's great for a still image you'll send to someone. It can't draw over apps while you're using them.

Zoom, Google Meet and Teams annotation (during calls only)

If you're in a call, the meeting app may have its own annotation tools. Zoom's Annotate works while you share your screen, and viewers can draw too. Google Meet's needs an eligible Google Workspace plan. These only work inside that app's meeting. How to draw during Zoom, Meet and Teams calls.

Questions

What's the best free app to draw on a Mac's screen?

For drawing while you present, JellyDraw: its free version has no time limit and includes the pen, highlighter, eraser, whiteboard and drawings that follow their window. For zooming in with a little drawing, ZoomIt for Mac. For a free app that also runs on Windows and Linux, DrawPen.

Can I draw on my Mac's screen without installing anything?

Not live. macOS Markup lets you draw on a screenshot, PDF or image after you capture it. To draw over your apps while you present, you need an app, or your meeting app's own tools during a call.

Is JellyDraw really free?

Yes. The free version never expires and has no watermark. It includes the pen, highlighter, eraser, select and move, undo and redo, whiteboard and blackboard, and window anchoring. Pro is optional: $9.99 once for shapes, text, export and accent colors.

Are Presentify, Overlaid and Scribbble free?

They're free to try, not free to keep. Scribbble is free to download and try with a $12 one-time license, Overlaid is $4.99 after a 7-day trial, and Presentify is $14.99 on the Mac App Store or through a Setapp subscription with a 7-day trial.

Do free screen drawing apps work in Zoom, Meet and Teams?

Yes, when you share your entire screen rather than a single window or tab. Step-by-step guide.

Which free app works on an Intel Mac?

ZoomIt for Mac supports Intel and Apple Silicon on macOS 14 or later. JellyDraw needs Apple Silicon, and DrawPen's download is for Apple Silicon.
